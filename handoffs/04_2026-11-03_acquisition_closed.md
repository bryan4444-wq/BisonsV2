## 3. Final negotiated Irsay family terms

The acquisition incorporates the commercial principles negotiated with Carlie Irsay-Gordon, Casey Foyt, Kalen Jackson, and their legal representatives.

### Family ownership and future exit

The Irsay family retains 30%, worth $2.04 billion at the closing valuation.

The agreement establishes a milestone-based opportunity for the family to sell its remaining equity.

The contemplated milestone framework includes completion of the ownership transaction, formal relocation approval, and the first regular-season home game in Oklahoma City.

When an eligible sale window opens, the purchase priority is:

1. Bryan Luong receives the first opportunity to acquire the offered interest.
2. Existing Oklahoma minority investors receive the next opportunity.
3. If neither purchases, the family may seek qualified outside investors, subject to required approvals.

The valuation is to be based on a fair-market mechanism rather than permanently fixing the franchise at $6.8 billion. Independent valuation procedures address disputes.

Recurring exit opportunities are part of the agreed framework. Specific exercise deadlines, appraisal procedures, and notice periods were not established in the visible negotiations.

Bryan also offered to purchase the entire Irsay interest during the original transaction. Carlie declined to make an immediate full-exit decision, preferring to retain 30% initially.

## 4. Employee transition protections

Bryan established an unusually substantial transition package for the Colts' Indianapolis employees.

Employees who remain eligible and in good standing through the 2026 season will receive:

- A fully funded relocation package if offered and accepting a position in Oklahoma City.
- Six months of severance if they do not relocate and their employment ends as part of the transition.
- Continued recognition of applicable contractual and employment obligations.

Normal turnover, legitimate performance-based terminations, and organizational restructuring remain possible.

The agreement is not a lifetime employment guarantee. Its purpose is to prevent employees from being abandoned financially during the franchise's relocation.

The Irsay family also requested safeguards against dismissals made solely to avoid these obligations.

## 5. Indianapolis farewell and community commitments

Bryan acknowledged that the Colts' departure would leave a significant gap in Indianapolis.

His negotiated commitment is to return 100% of the profits from authorized farewell merchandise and memorabilia sales to existing Colts community partners and selected local charities.

Eligible programs may include commemorative merchandise, authenticated team equipment, game-worn memorabilia, and legally saleable franchise-owned assets.

The arrangement requires clearly defined direct costs, transparent accounting, and appropriate beneficiary selection.

It does not authorize the sale of stadium property the franchise does not own.

The purpose is to give Indianapolis supporters a meaningful farewell while ensuring the proceeds benefit the communities that supported the Colts.
