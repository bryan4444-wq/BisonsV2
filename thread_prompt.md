## **1. Timeline and major developments**

### **Tuesday, February 23 — The Bijan Robinson blockbuster**

Bryan and the football operations staff explored acquiring Bijan Robinson from Atlanta.

After negotiations with Falcons GM Ian Cunningham, Atlanta ownership conditionally approved:

| Oklahoma City sends            | Atlanta sends      |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Omarion Hampton, HB            | Bijan Robinson, HB |
| Brandon Aiyuk, WR              |                    |
| 2027 first-round pick No. 22   |                    |
| 2028 fourth-round pick No. 112 |                    |

**Trade status at last explicit confirmation:** Finalized February 24 after Hampton passed his physical, as established in Archive 2.0.0.

Bijan's Madden profile at the time was 97 OVR, X-Factor, age 25, with three contract years remaining.

Bryan personally informed Hampton. Shane Steichen personally informed Aiyuk at Bryan's request.

Hampton was initially hurt and questioned whether his knee injury motivated the trade. Bryan reassured him that Atlanta valued him and that acquiring the league's best running back was an opportunity Oklahoma City couldn't pass up.

Aiyuk understood the business decision but was disappointed he never received a complete offseason to establish himself with Herbert and Marvin Harrison Jr.

### **Wednesday, February 24 — Farewell gathering**

Hampton and Aiyuk visited Lake Hefner headquarters before traveling to Atlanta aboard Bryan's personal jet.

Present were Justin Herbert, Marvin Harrison Jr., Bobby Wagner, Shane Steichen, Scot McCloughan, Pete Carroll, and Bryan.

The atmosphere mixed disappointment, appreciation, and humor. Hampton joked that Bryan had traded two players for one running back.

**Continuity note:** Archive 2.0.0 establishes the completed trade and passed physical.

## **2. Free agency — Bryan's offensive strategy**

Bryan rejected the idea of spending approximately $30 million annually on George Pickens, despite acknowledging his talent.

The preferred approach was to acquire affordable, complementary receivers rather than another expensive star alongside Marvin Harrison Jr.

Bryan particularly wanted Tank Dell, with Josh Downs and Tre Tucker initially considered alternatives. Ultimately, **both Dell and Downs were signed**.

At tight end, Bryan evaluated Cole Kmet against T.J. Hockenson, then signed both. John Bates was also signed specifically to convert to fullback.

The resulting offensive plan emphasizes versatility, additional receiving options for Herbert, and stronger personnel flexibility.
