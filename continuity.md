## At 28, Bryan Luong Has Bought the Indianapolis Colts. His First Day Left the NFL With More Questions Than Answers.

*A self-made engineering billionaire, a Seahawks legend, an uncertain future in Indianapolis — and high school football highlights that have unexpectedly taken the internet by storm.*

INDIANAPOLIS — Bryan Luong stood before 43 journalists Wednesday afternoon as the NFL's newest controlling owner, looking remarkably comfortable for a 28-year-old whose first official press conference involved questions about franchise relocation, coaching security, and whether he was qualified to oversee a professional football organization.

By the time it ended, Luong had guaranteed that Shane Steichen would remain head coach through the 2026 season, endorsed retired linebacker Bobby Wagner as his right-hand man, declined to promise the Colts would remain in Indianapolis long-term, and inadvertently sent his old high school football highlights viral.

It was an unusual introduction to NFL ownership.

And perhaps a fitting one for an unusual owner.

### From a five-person engineering firm to NFL ownership

Luong founded Ascend, an information and communications technology engineering firm, in 2022.

What began as a small engineering operation grew through nine acquisitions across the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom.

By October 2026, Ascend carried an estimated enterprise valuation of $8 billion.

- Irsay family: 30%.
- Oklahoma investor syndicate: 19%.

Luong, however, holds controlling ownership and intends to serve as general manager while delegating substantial football responsibilities to Wagner.

### Bobby Wagner trades a historic pursuit for a front-office opportunity

Wagner's decision to join Luong may be the most intriguing football decision of the new regime.

The longtime linebacker stepped away from his playing career approximately 50 tackles short of the NFL's all-time career tackles record in this season's record book.

The two men face an immediate challenge: a Colts roster in transition.

Luong rejected the suggestion that Indianapolis is entering a deliberate rebuilding or tanking period.

Steichen's future beyond the season remains undecided.

Luong said his evaluation will focus on how Steichen develops players, handles adversity, and adapts to personnel changes rather than merely his win-loss record.

That distinction could prove important for a team whose roster has already experienced significant upheaval.

The most consequential exchange Wednesday had little to do with football.

Asked whether the Colts would still be in Indianapolis five years from now, Luong declined to guarantee it.

"While I can't guarantee you all five years in Indy, I can guarantee that our time here will be memorable."

Luong subsequently suggested the Colts could remain in Indianapolis for another decade.

No Colts relocation has been approved or announced.

The distinction is critical. A change in controlling ownership does not itself authorize a franchise to move, and any proposed relocation would require the NFL's established approval process.
