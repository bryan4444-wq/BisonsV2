The 28-Year-Old Who Refuses to Build Quietly: Inside the Oklahoma City Bisons’ First NFL Draft

By Daniel Mercer | ESPN Senior NFL Reporter · April 2027

The Bisons traded three picks to land their top corner, added three receivers to a crowded room and emerged with a B from Daniel Mercer.

OKLAHOMA CITY — There is a particular kind of confidence required to trade three draft selections for the right to move up four spots. There is another kind entirely when the man authorizing that trade is 28 years old, owns the franchise, and has never presided over an NFL Draft.

Bryan Luong possesses both.

On Saturday evening, Oklahoma City had completed its first draft class. Eight players, enough speed to make a track coach reconsider his recruiting budget, and an identity. Whether that identity translates into winning football remains an entirely different question.

The trade that defined the draft

Oklahoma City entered Thursday night holding No. 13 and left the first round with Clemson cornerback Ashton Hampton, selected ninth after sending Philadelphia Nos. 13, 81 and 144. It was expensive. It also might have been the smartest decision Luong made all weekend.

Hampton, 21, received a top-five evaluation from Oklahoma City's scouts. His reveal confirmed an 80 overall rating, 94 speed and 93 acceleration. In a secondary already featuring Byron Murphy Jr., Hampton gives Oklahoma City another premium talent. Philadelphia selected a corner with the pick Oklahoma City surrendered, which helps explain the urgency even though the Eagles' preference remains unknown.

The receiver room is suddenly crowded

The Bisons already had Marvin Harrison Jr. and had signed Tank Dell and Josh Downs. They drafted three more receivers. Alabama's Ryan Coleman-Williams went No. 35 with 94 speed and 94 acceleration, well ahead of his true-value ranking of No. 74. Nyck Harbor went No. 112 with 97 speed and 96 acceleration, but his hands and route execution need work. Oklahoma's Isaiah Sategna, selected No. 191, brings 92 speed and a chance to compete for return duties.

Speed does not automatically produce separation. Athleticism does not guarantee reliable hands. And six receivers cannot all be featured simultaneously. Justin Herbert has more weapons than he did before free agency. Whether he has a coherent receiving hierarchy is still unanswered.

The defense has a direction

Clemson edge defender Will Heldt went No. 49 and Penn State linebacker Tony Rojas went No. 58. Heldt arrived at 73 overall, Rojas at 74. They join a defense reinforced by Yaya Diaby, DeForest Buckner, Leonard Williams, DeMarvion Overshown and Jevon Holland.

The most consequential move may have been on the sideline. Luong dismissed Lou Anarumo after a disagreement over the switch to a 4–3 and hired former Seahawks linebacker K.J. Wright. Wright brings playing experience and familiarity with four-man fronts. Now he must show that he can coordinate an entire defense. The change gives the staff a clear direction and puts pressure on it to deliver.

The Oklahoma connection

At No. 176 the Bisons took John Mateer, a 66-overall quarterback with 86 speed and 87 acceleration. His mobility gives the coaching staff a different developmental option. Passing velocity and intermediate consistency remain concerns. His Oklahoma ties will appeal to local fans, as will Sategna's, but the football case will depend on what each can do in camp. A sixth-round gamble is affordable. Recognizing when it has failed is the harder part.

The $100 million question

The draft followed a blockbuster for Bijan Robinson and significant spending in free agency. Even after rookie contracts, the Madden screen displayed approximately $104 million in remaining cap room. Robinson, Herbert, Harrison, the defensive veterans and the rookies must eventually fit into a long-term plan. For now the Bisons have flexibility. The challenge will be preserving it.

Overall grade: B. Best selection: Ashton Hampton, CB, No. 9.

Hampton could start immediately. Rojas offers useful linebacker depth, Heldt adds a young edge defender, and Coleman-Williams and Harbor give the offensive staff options. Oklahoma City also spent meaningful capital moving four spots, reached for several offensive prospects and left questions along the offensive line.

Bobby Wagner leads football operations. Scot McCloughan directs scouting. Shane Steichen coaches the team. Pete Carroll serves as senior adviser. The real test of Luong's leadership will be whether those voices retain their influence when their evaluations conflict with his instincts.

The 28-year-old owner has made his selections. Now comes the part he cannot purchase, negotiate or trade his way around.

The players have to perform.

Back at Bisons headquarters

“B? Mercer gave us a B?” Scot asks. His own grade was a B-plus. Bobby says Mercer makes some fair points, and K.J. Wright agrees that the staff has to make the defensive transition work.

Bobby turns the room toward undrafted prospects, starting with offensive line, defensive depth and special teams. Scot still has about forty names on his board. Offers and signings are still ahead.
