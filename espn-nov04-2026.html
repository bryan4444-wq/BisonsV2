## At 28, Bryan Luong Has Bought the Indianapolis Colts. His First Day Left the NFL With More Questions Than Answers.

*A self-made engineering billionaire, a Seahawks legend, an uncertain future in Indianapolis — and high school football highlights that have unexpectedly taken the internet by storm.*

INDIANAPOLIS — Bryan Luong stood before 43 journalists Wednesday afternoon as the NFL's newest controlling owner, looking remarkably comfortable for a 28-year-old whose first official press conference involved questions about franchise relocation, coaching security, and whether he was qualified to oversee a professional football organization.

By the time it ended, Luong had guaranteed that Shane Steichen would remain head coach through the 2026 season, endorsed retired linebacker Bobby Wagner as his right-hand man, declined to promise the Colts would remain in Indianapolis long-term, and inadvertently sent his old high school football highlights viral.

It was an unusual introduction to NFL ownership.

And perhaps a fitting one for an unusual owner.

### From a five-person engineering firm to NFL ownership

Luong founded Ascend, an information and communications technology engineering firm, in 2022.

What began as a small engineering operation grew through nine acquisitions across the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom.

By October 2026, Ascend carried an estimated enterprise valuation of $8 billion.

Luong's personal wealth grew alongside the company, aided by a substantial lottery windfall early in his entrepreneurial career.

On November 3, Luong and an Oklahoma-based investment group completed their acquisition of a combined 70% interest in the Indianapolis Colts at a $6.8 billion franchise valuation.

Ownership:

- Bryan Luong: 51%.
- Irsay family: 30%.
- Oklahoma investor syndicate: 19%.

The Oklahoma investment group includes interests associated with Love's, former NBA star Blake Griffin, Oklahoma City Thunder guard Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, and several prominent former NFL players.

Luong, however, holds controlling ownership and intends to serve as general manager while delegating substantial football responsibilities to Wagner.

### Bobby Wagner trades a historic pursuit for a front-office opportunity

Wagner's decision to join Luong may be the most intriguing football decision of the new regime.

The longtime linebacker stepped away from his playing career approximately 50 tackles short of the NFL's all-time career tackles record in this season's record book.

He has never previously held a senior NFL front-office position.

Luong is unconcerned.

"I've always admired Bobby from his playing days, his leadership, and his pursuit of education off the field," Luong said. "He's an incredibly intelligent man with years in the NFL. There's not a better person I'd want as my right-hand man."

Wagner acknowledged the unconventional nature of the arrangement.

"When a twenty-eight-year-old billionaire calls and says he wants you to help run an NFL team, you have some questions!" Wagner joked.

But Wagner also emphasized that playing experience alone does not qualify someone to run an organization.

He described the importance of experienced scouts, personnel executives, and contract specialists.

The two men face an immediate challenge: a Colts roster in transition.

### A reload, not a rebuild

Luong rejected the suggestion that Indianapolis is entering a deliberate rebuilding or tanking period.

"It's not a rebuild nor a tank. It's a reload," he told reporters.

He acknowledged that roster turnover is coming, while promising no coaching changes for the remainder of 2026.

Steichen's future beyond the season remains undecided.

Luong said his evaluation will focus on how Steichen develops players, handles adversity, and adapts to personnel changes rather than merely his win-loss record.

That distinction could prove important for a team whose roster has already experienced significant upheaval.

### The Indianapolis question

The most consequential exchange Wednesday had little to do with football.

Asked whether the Colts would still be in Indianapolis five years from now, Luong declined to guarantee it.

Later, ESPN's Mina Kimes pressed him on the emotional impact of potentially losing a professional sports franchise.

Luong invoked the Seattle SuperSonics, whose departure from his childhood region left a lasting impression.

He argued that the Colts' ownership transition is fundamentally different from the stadium dispute that preceded Seattle's NBA departure.

Yet his most memorable response may also have been his most unsettling for Indianapolis fans.

"While I can't guarantee you all five years in Indy, I can guarantee that our time here will be memorable."

Luong subsequently suggested the Colts could remain in Indianapolis for another decade.

He also said employees would be offered relocation assistance if the organization eventually moved, while those unable to relocate would receive compensation.

No Colts relocation has been approved or announced.

The distinction is critical. A change in controlling ownership does not itself authorize a franchise to move, and any proposed relocation would require the NFL's established approval process.

Still, Luong's unwillingness to offer a long-term commitment ensures that the franchise's geographic future will remain a major storyline.

### The most unexpected development: Hudl

Perhaps the strangest moment of the afternoon began when Luong casually invited reporters to look up his high school football highlights.

The 5-foot-7 former defensive tackle and fullback had described receiving interest from Division II and Division III programs before family circumstances altered his college plans.

Independent Colts reporter Daniel Mercer located the footage during the press conference.

What he discovered prompted an audible expletive that briefly interrupted proceedings.

One clip showed Luong bulldozing through three defenders on a seven-yard touchdown run.

Another showed him throwing a considerably taller offensive lineman off his feet on the way to a sack.

Wagner, watching the footage, reportedly demanded that the play be replayed.

Within hours, the video had exploded across football social media.

**Hudl views: 99 before the press conference; 79,121 by Wednesday evening. An increase of 79,022 views, approximately 799 times the original count.**

Social media users quickly began circulating both plays, with some joking that the Colts' new owner should be evaluated as a short-yardage fullback.

Others questioned whether the attention surrounding his athletic background was distracting from the much more serious questions about Indianapolis' future.

For Luong, however, the moment offered something few billionaire NFL owners possess: evidence of having played the game, albeit at the high school level.

It does not establish his qualifications as a general manager.

But it helps explain why football appears to be more than a financial investment for him.

### What comes next?

Luong's first day provided several concrete commitments.

Steichen will finish the season. Wagner will help oversee football operations. Significant roster evaluations are coming. And the organization says employees will be treated fairly if major changes occur.

Other questions remain unanswered.

How will Luong's unusual owner-general manager structure function? Who will join Wagner in the front office? How quickly will the roster change?

And most importantly for Indianapolis: where will the Colts play five years from now?

Luong has agreed to additional interviews Friday, including a conversation with ESPN's Mina Kimes.

For now, Indianapolis has a new owner, an uncertain future, and an unexpected viral football star.

The NFL's newest billionaire has certainly made an entrance.
