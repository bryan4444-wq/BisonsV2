Oklahoma City’s $197 Million Warning Shot: Bisons Land Bijan Robinson in Blockbuster Falcons Trade

By Daniel Mercer | ESPN Senior NFL Reporter · February 24, 2027 · 4:30 PM ET

OKLAHOMA CITY — Bryan Luong has owned an NFL franchise for less than four months. He has already relocated it, reshaped its front office, acquired Justin Herbert and Marvin Harrison Jr., and overseen the final chapter of Indianapolis Colts football. On Wednesday he made perhaps his boldest football decision yet.

The Oklahoma City Bisons acquired superstar running back Bijan Robinson from the Atlanta Falcons. The deal was finalized Wednesday afternoon after Omarion Hampton passed his physical. Robinson, 25, arrives with three years remaining on his extension. Oklahoma City retains the No. 13 overall pick.

The price of acquiring a superstar

Hampton rushed for 983 yards and 13 touchdowns in 2026 despite a late-season partial ACL tear. Atlanta adds an experienced receiver opposite Drake London in Aiyuk, and the No. 22 pick returns to its original team. Oklahoma City gets one of football’s most complete offensive weapons, at the cost of two contributors and significant draft capital. Hampton could become a franchise back himself, and Aiyuk’s departure leaves an immediate opening at receiver.

Herbert threw for 3,299 yards and 17 touchdowns across his combined stops last season. Harrison recorded 826 receiving yards, and Warren emerged as one of the team’s most promising young players. Robinson gives Shane Steichen a mismatch in both the rushing and passing games. The Bisons still need to replace Aiyuk, protect Herbert and turn individual talent into a cohesive offense. The Colts finished 7–1 after a 2–7 start and still missed the playoffs.

The owner who refuses to wait

Luong was personally involved in talks with Falcons GM Ian Cunningham before Atlanta ownership authorized the deal. The teams reached a conditional agreement Tuesday. Hampton’s medical evaluation cleared the way for completion Wednesday.

Luong personally informed Hampton, and Steichen contacted Aiyuk. Both players visited Lake Hefner headquarters Wednesday morning to say goodbye before traveling to Atlanta aboard Luong’s private aircraft. That departure offered a look at how Luong and his leadership group, including Bobby Wagner, Scot McCloughan and Pete Carroll, handle the human side of aggressive roster decisions.

The $197 million question

The Madden trade screen shows $197.13 million in Oklahoma City cap space after the trade, subject to routine accounting adjustments. With free agency opening Thursday, receiver options include George Pickens, Parker Washington, Michael Pittman Jr. and Rashee Rice. DeForest Buckner, Tremaine Edmunds and Jevon Holland are among the defensive options as the team transitions to a 4–3.

A franchise without patience

The Bisons have yet to play a game under their new name. They already have a new headquarters near Lake Hefner, a superstar running back and an owner determined to accelerate the process. The next test is whether those choices can produce sustained winning. That will depend on depth, protection, coaching and health over a full season.

The Bisons have acquired one of football’s premier offensive talents. Now they must prove they can build a championship-caliber team around him. And with free agency less than 24 hours away, the league should probably keep its phones nearby.
